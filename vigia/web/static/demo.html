01
Only look for what
can happen here

Each camera is told what it overlooks. A camera on a ridge watches for smoke. A camera on a street watches for floodwater and stranded vehicles. Nobody looks for drowning on a hillside.

Running every detector on every camera would cost four times the computer, for no extra safety.

02
Ask a specialist

Each hazard has its own detector, built by people who know that problem best — wildfire researchers, coastguard datasets, earthquake response teams. We did not reinvent any of them.

One model trained on everything would be worse at all of it, and retraining one hazard could quietly break another.

03
Wait before
believing it
Our contribution

A detector shouts on every frame. VIGÍA checks whether the thing is still there a moment later. Smoke keeps rising; a glint on water does not. That one idea removes most false alarms.

1× ignored 2× still waiting 3× wake someone

Nobody else ships this. It costs about two seconds and removes three quarters of the false alarms.

Worth knowing

Waiting is not free. Holding out for a third sighting means an alert arrives one to three minutes later on a camera that takes a picture every minute. On our test fires it never lost one — but the trade is real, and we report both halves of it.

Teach it a hazard

Give it pictures where someone has already marked what is in them. It learns from those and produces a detector the system can use.

Training runs in short stages so a laptop can finish it, and a stage that is interrupted resumes where it stopped. Starting a run from this screen is deliberately not wired up: a live demonstration should show a finished one, not thirty-five minutes of a progress bar.
